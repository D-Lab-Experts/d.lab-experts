  <h2 id="contexto">1. Why this study matters for your business</h2>

  <p>The global games market is projected to surpass <span class="hl-lilac">US$ 212 billion by 2026</span>, according to Newzoo. In Brazil, <span class="hl-lilac">70.1% of the population played some kind of game in 2023</span>, per Pesquisa Game Brasil. This is no longer a niche. It is mass consumption.</p>

  <p>But it is consumption that has changed in nature. Edelman research as far back as 2007 showed that <span class="hl-bg-blue">85% of consumers prefer socially responsible brands</span> and that 70% will pay a premium for it. That behavior is no longer news. What was not yet well mapped is how it manifests inside the Brazilian gaming market, and whether it actually influences purchase decisions or is just nice talk in a survey.</p>

  <p>That was the gap this D.Lab Experts research tried to fill. Instead of asking <em>"do you prefer responsible brands?"</em> (the answer is always yes), we asked more practical things: <span class="hl-white">do you feel closer to those brands? Do you believe the intent is real? Would you switch brands because of it?</span></p>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Why you should read this</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">If you're responsible for brand, marketing or ESG, this study delivers three things that matter to your work:</div>
    <ul>
      <li>A clear number for the return on affirmative campaigns in the Brazilian gamer audience.</li>
      <li>An explanation for why the audience is skeptical, and what to do about it.</li>
      <li>Criteria to distinguish an action that will generate brand value from one that will generate accusations of opportunism.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h2 id="metodo">2. How the research was conducted</h2>

  <p>This was a <span class="term-def" data-def="A study where data is collected at a single point in time, like a snapshot. Different from a longitudinal study, which follows the same people over time.">cross-sectional</span> observational study with an anonymous online questionnaire. The protocol followed STROBE guidelines for cross-sectional studies (Von Elm et al., 2007), the internationally accepted standard for this type of collection.</p>

  <h3>How collection worked</h3>
  <p>Google Forms questionnaire distributed across Brazilian gamer communities (Facebook, Reddit r/gamesEcultura, Discord). Recruitment was by <span class="term-def" data-def="A way to recruit respondents by offering the questionnaire in existing communities, without random sampling. Fast and cheap, but can over-represent the most active members.">convenience sampling</span> and <span class="term-def" data-def="When one respondent invites others to also respond, expanding the sample like a snowball.">snowball</span>. Collection period: January to March 2023.</p>

  <h3>What was measured</h3>
  <p>20 questions in total: 10 demographic, 6 about perception and behavior (Yes/No answers), 1 open-ended question on remembered brands, and 3 structural items. The 6 key questions were:</p>

  <h3>Who was classified as a minority gamer</h3>
  <p>A respondent entered the "minority" group if they met at least one of these criteria: a gender different from cis-male, a race/color different from white, a sexual orientation different from heterosexual, or the presence of a disability. The definition follows Siqueira and Castro (2017).</p>

  <h3>Statistical tests used (and why each one)</h3>
  <p>The analysis used six complementary tests, each with a distinct function:</p>
  <div style="display:flex;flex-wrap:wrap;gap:8px;margin:14px 0 22px">
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Verifies whether the proportion of Yes answers in a question is statistically different from what would be expected by chance (50%).">Binomial test</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Non-parametric version of the t-test. Compares whether the mean of a variable differs from a reference value, without assuming normal distribution.">Wilcoxon signed-rank</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between a binary (Yes/No) and a continuous variable. Ranges from -1 to +1.">Point-biserial correlation</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Measures whether two variables move together, even when the relationship is non-linear. Ranges from -1 (opposite directions) to +1 (same direction).">Spearman's rho</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Compares whether two groups have statistically different distributions, without assuming normality.">Mann-Whitney U</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Calculates the exact probability of an observed difference in small (2x2) tables. More precise than chi-square when the sample is small.">Fisher's exact</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="A model that estimates the chance of an event (e.g., switching brands) happening based on multiple factors at once, controlling for cross-influences.">Logistic regression</span>
  </div>
  <p>Adopted significance level: <span class="term-def" data-def="α (alpha) is the threshold beyond which we consider a result statistically significant. 0.05 means we accept a 5% chance that the result occurred by chance.">α = 0.05</span>. Software: Python 3.12+ (pandas, scipy, statsmodels).</p>

  <h2 id="paradoxo">5. The sincerity paradox</h2>

  <p>How can 87.8% believe campaigns work but only 16.3% believe the brand is sincere? The answer: the Brazilian gamer audience separated the two things. They understand every brand has a commercial objective. They accept that. What they evaluate is whether the action <span class="hl-white">delivers measurable results</span>. It is not an evaluation of intent. It is an evaluation of impact.</p>

  <p>This finding has theoretical support. Porter and Kramer (2006) argue in <em>Strategy and Society</em> that corporate social responsibility generates value even when strategically motivated. What our research adds is the Brazilian empirical evidence: gamer consumers reached the same conclusion intuitively.</p>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Business insight · The sincerity paradox</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Stop trying to look authentic. Start delivering measurable results.</div>
    <p>If your strategy depends on the audience believing in the "soul of the brand", you are losing. The audience already decided not to believe. But it <strong>is willing to reward</strong> brands that deliver results, even knowing there is commercial interest behind them.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Replace emotional institutional film with auditable impact reports.</li>
      <li>Show how much was invested, who benefited, what was the measured result.</li>
      <li>Give numbers, dates, names of partner organizations, verifiable evidence.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h2 id="minoria">6. The amplified minority effect</h2>

  <p>When we split the sample between the minority and non-minority groups, the most important difference appears in <span class="hl-white">willingness to switch brands for social commitment</span>. The numbers:</p>

    <div class="data-card-title">"Yes" response by group (hover to compare)</div>

  <p>The most expressive difference is in <span class="hl-bg-lilac">Q6 Brand switching</span>: <span class="hl-green">72.9% in the minority group against 43.6% in the non-minority group</span>. That is 29.3 percentage points. In statistical-chance terms, calculated by Fisher's exact test, it equals an <span class="term-def" data-def="Odds Ratio (OR) is the ratio between the chances of an event happening in two groups. OR = 3.48 means that for every non-minority gamer willing to switch brands, there are 3.48 minority gamers in the same position.">Odds Ratio of 3.48</span> (p = 0.006).</p>

  <p>In business language: <span class="hl-white">for every non-minority gamer willing to switch brands, there are nearly three and a half more minority gamers in the same "ready to switch" group</span>. That is the actual size of the opportunity.</p>

    <div class="callout-body">Affirmative campaigns in the gaming market <strong>are not just socially desirable</strong>. They are <strong>commercially strategic</strong>, especially for brands that want to capture diverse audiences (which are also the fastest-growing audiences in the market).</div>
  </div>

  <h2 id="implicacoes">9. Practical implications for your brand</h2>

  <blockquote><p>Affirmative campaigns in the gaming market are not symbolic gesture. They are measurable competitive advantage.</p></blockquote>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 1</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Focus on impact, not on narrative</div>
    <p>Brazilian gamers are skeptical about motivation (16.3% believe in sincerity), but they are receptive to results (87.8% perceive impact). Invest in actions with measurable, auditable results, not in generic values communication. Every brand piece should answer one simple question: <span class="hl-white">what number did I improve?</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 2</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Prioritize minority audiences</div>
    <p>With a 72.9% switching intent against 43.6% in the non-minority group, the conversion potential is significantly higher. Cases like the Xbox Adaptive Controller and Itaú's Taça das Favelas are references spontaneously cited by Brazilian gamers. <span class="hl-white">That is the kind of program that turns into long-term brand capital.</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 3</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Invest in consistency, not in one-offs</div>
    <p>The low perception of sincerity indicates that one-off actions read as opportunism. Brands that maintain ongoing programs (FURIA with Casa 1, Riot with regular fundraising) accumulate brand capital cumulatively. <span class="hl-white">One campaign per year is wasted budget. A continuous three-year program is investment.</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 4</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Show up with product, not just communication</div>
    <p>The most-remembered examples are not advertising campaigns. They are products (Xbox Adaptive Controller), infrastructure (Valorant Game Changers) and platforms (Taça das Favelas). Think about where your brand can <span class="hl-white">become part of the experience</span>, and not just an external sponsor.</p>
  </div>

  <h2 id="limitacoes">10. Limits of this research</h2>

  <p>Every study has limits. This one has eight. Worth reading carefully before extrapolating the results:</p>

  <div class="data-card">
    <div class="data-card-title">What this research cannot claim</div>
    <table class="stat-table">
      <thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Limitation</th><th>Implication</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Cross-sectional design</strong></td><td>Does not allow causal claims. We identified associations, not cause and effect.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>2</td><td><strong>N = 98</strong> (target was 200)</td><td>Reduced statistical power for subgroups. Treat as a pilot study.</td></tr>
