  <h2 id="contexto">1. Why this research matters for your business</h2>

  <p>The global gaming market is expected to surpass <span class="hl-lilac">US$212 billion by 2026</span>, according to Newzoo. In Brazil, <span class="hl-lilac">70.1% of the population played some kind of game in 2023</span>, according to Pesquisa Game Brasil. This is no longer a niche. It's mass consumption.</p>

  <p>But it's a kind of consumption that has changed in nature. An Edelman study back in 2007 showed that <span class="hl-bg-blue">85% of consumers prefer socially responsible brands</span> and that 70% are willing to pay a premium for it. This behavior is no longer news. What still hadn't been well mapped is how it manifests within the gaming market in Brazil, and whether it actually influences purchase decisions or is just nice words in a survey.</p>

  <p>That was the gap this D.Lab Research study set out to fill. Instead of asking <em>"do you prefer responsible brands?"</em> (the answer is always yes), we asked more practical questions: <span class="hl-white">do you feel closer to these brands? Do you believe the intent is genuine? Would you switch brands because of it?</span></p>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Why you should read this</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">If you're responsible for brand, marketing, or ESG, this research delivers three things that matter for your work:</div>
    <ul>
      <li>A clear number for the return on affirmative campaigns among Brazilian gamers.</li>
      <li>An explanation for why the audience is skeptical, and what to do about it.</li>
      <li>Criteria to distinguish an action that will build brand value from one that will draw accusations of opportunism.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h2 id="metodo">2. How the research was conducted</h2>

  <p>It was a <span class="term-def" data-def="A study where data is collected at a single point in time, like a snapshot. Different from a longitudinal study, which follows the same people over time.">cross-sectional study</span>, observational, with an anonymous online questionnaire. The protocol followed the STROBE guidelines for cross-sectional studies (Von Elm et al., 2007), which is the internationally accepted standard for this type of data collection.</p>

  <h3>How data collection worked</h3>
  <p>A Google Forms questionnaire, distributed across Brazilian gamer communities (Facebook, Reddit r/gamesEcultura, Discord). Recruitment was by <span class="term-def" data-def="A way of recruiting respondents by offering the questionnaire in existing communities, without random selection. It's fast and cheap, but it can over-represent those who are most active in those communities.">convenience sampling</span> and <span class="term-def" data-def="When a respondent refers others to respond as well, expanding the sample like a snowball.">snowball</span>. Collection period: January to March 2023.</p>

  <h3>What was measured</h3>
  <p>20 questions in total: 10 demographic, 6 on perception and behavior (with Yes/No answers), 1 open-ended question about recalled brands, and 3 structural items. The 6 key questions were:</p>

  <h3>Who was classified as a minoritized gamer</h3>
  <p>A respondent entered the "minoritized" group if they met at least one of these criteria: a gender other than cis man, a race/color other than white, a sexual orientation other than heterosexual, or the presence of a disability. The definition follows Siqueira and Castro (2017).</p>

  <p>The analysis used six complementary tests, each with a function:</p>
  <div style="display:flex;flex-wrap:wrap;gap:8px;margin:14px 0 22px">
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Checks whether the proportion of Yes answers to a question is statistically different from what would be expected by chance (50%).">Binomial test</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="A non-parametric version of the t-test. Compares whether the mean of a variable differs from a reference value, without assuming the data follow a normal distribution.">Wilcoxon signed-rank</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between a binary variable (Yes/No) and a continuous one. Ranges from -1 to +1.">Point-biserial correlation</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Measures whether two variables move together, even when the relationship is not linear. Ranges from -1 (move in opposite directions) to +1 (move together).">Spearman's rho</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Compares whether two groups have statistically different distributions, without assuming normality of the data.">Mann-Whitney U</span>
    <span class="method-badge term-def" data-def="Calculates the exact probability of an observed difference in small tables (2x2). More precise than chi-square when the sample is small.">Fisher's exact</span>

  <h2 id="paradoxo">5. The sincerity paradox</h2>

  <p>How can 87.8% believe campaigns work but only 16.3% believe the brand is sincere? The answer is: the Brazilian gamer audience has separated the two things. They understand that every brand has a commercial goal. They accept that. What they evaluate is whether the action <span class="hl-white">delivers measurable results</span>. It's not an assessment of intent. It's an assessment of impact.</p>

  <p>This finding has theoretical support. Porter and Kramer (2006) argue in <em>Strategy and Society</em> that corporate social responsibility generates value even when strategically motivated. What our research adds is the Brazilian empirical evidence: the gamer consumer arrived at that same conclusion intuitively.</p>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Business insight · The sincerity paradox</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Stop trying to look authentic. Start delivering measurable results.</div>
    <p>If your strategy depends on the audience believing in the "soul of the brand," you're losing. The audience has already decided it doesn't believe. But it <strong>is willing to reward</strong> brands that deliver results, even knowing there's a commercial interest behind them.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Replace the emotional brand film with an auditable impact report.</li>
      <li>Show how much was invested, who benefited, and the measured result.</li>
      <li>Give numbers, dates, the names of partner organizations, verifiable evidence.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h2 id="minoria">6. The amplified minority effect</h2>

  <p>When we split the sample between the minoritized and non-minoritized groups, the most important difference appears in the <span class="hl-white">willingness to switch brands over social commitment</span>. The numbers:</p>

  <div class="data-card">
    <div class="data-card-title">"Yes" answers by group (hover to compare)</div>

  <p>The most striking difference is in <span class="hl-bg-lilac">P6 Brand switching</span>: <span class="hl-green">72.9% in the minoritized group versus 43.6% in the non-minoritized group</span>. That's 29.3 percentage points. In terms of statistical odds, calculated using Fisher's exact test, it's equivalent to an <span class="term-def" data-def="The Odds Ratio (OR) is the ratio between the odds of an event happening in two groups. OR = 3.48 means that for each non-minoritized gamer willing to switch brands, there are 3.48 minoritized gamers in the same position.">Odds Ratio of 3.48</span> (p = 0.006).</p>

  <p>In business terms: <span class="hl-white">for each non-minoritized gamer willing to switch brands, there are nearly three and a half times more minoritized gamers in the same "ready to switch" group</span>. That's the real size of the opportunity.</p>

  <h2 id="implicacoes">9. Practical implications for your brand</h2>

  <blockquote><p>Affirmative campaigns in the gaming market are not a symbolic gesture. They are a measurable competitive advantage.</p></blockquote>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 1</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Focus on impact, not on narrative</div>
    <p>Brazilian gamers are skeptical about motivation (16.3% believe in sincerity), but they are receptive to results (87.8% perceive impact). Invest in actions with measurable, auditable results, not in generic values-based messaging. Every piece of brand communication should answer a simple question: <span class="hl-white">which number did I improve?</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 2</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Prioritize minoritized audiences</div>
    <p>With 72.9% willingness to switch brands versus 43.6% in the non-minoritized group, the conversion potential is significantly higher. Cases like the Xbox adaptive controller and Itaú's Taça das Favelas are references spontaneously cited by Brazilian gamers. <span class="hl-white">That's the kind of program that becomes long-term brand capital.</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 3</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Invest in consistency, not one-off actions</div>
    <p>The low perception of sincerity indicates that one-off actions are read as opportunism. Brands that maintain ongoing programs (FURIA with Casa 1, Riot with regular fundraising) build brand capital cumulatively. <span class="hl-white">One campaign a year is a waste of budget. A continuous three-year program is an investment.</span></p>
  </div>

  <div class="biz-insight">
    <div class="biz-insight-eyebrow">Recommendation 4</div>
    <div class="biz-insight-title">Make your presence felt with product, not just communication</div>
    <p>The most-remembered examples are not advertising campaigns. They are products (the Xbox adaptive controller), infrastructure (Valorant Game Changers), and platforms (Taça das Favelas). Think about where your brand can <span class="hl-white">become part of the experience</span>, rather than just an outside sponsor.</p>
  </div>

  <h2 id="limitacoes">10. Limits of this research</h2>

  <p>Every study has limits. This one has eight. It's worth reading carefully before extrapolating the results:</p>

  <div class="data-card">
    <div class="data-card-title">What this research cannot claim</div>
    <table class="stat-table">
      <thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Limitation</th><th>Implication</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Cross-sectional design</strong></td><td>Does not allow claims of causality. We identified associations, not cause and effect.</td></tr>

        <tr><td>5</td><td><strong>Stated intent</strong></td><td>Intent to switch is not the same as actually switching. Purchase behavior needs additional validation.</td></tr>
