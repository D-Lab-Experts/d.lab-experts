    <span class="section-number fi">01 · The problem</span>
    <h2 class="section-title fi">Why SMBs make critical decisions without analytical structure</h2>
    <p class="section-lead fi">Business mortality in Brazil is a structural problem, but rarely treated as a data and decision-process problem.</p>

    <p>Brazil has <span class="highlight">22.4 million small and micro enterprises</span>, according to IBGE. Every year, roughly 1.3 million new businesses open. Of those, nearly a third don't survive the first year. Public debate usually focuses on taxation, credit and bureaucracy. Those are real factors, but they miss a structural cause: the <span class="highlight">quality of decision in the founding and operation of the business</span>.</p>

    <p>SEBRAE research identified that among the main reasons for early closure are lack of prior planning, insufficient market knowledge and, crucially, <span class="highlight-blue">expansion, positioning and operational decisions taken without data</span>. Pricing, audience choice, competitor analysis, entry timing: all intelligence problems before they become operational ones.</p>

    <p>What catches the eye is that <span class="highlight">market ignorance, mispricing and unforeseen competition are, in essence, data problems</span>. The entrepreneur erred because they didn't have (or didn't use) information to decide correctly.</p>

    <p>The question guiding this research: <strong>is this gap avoidable? And at what cost?</strong></p>

    <span class="section-number fi">02 · The intelligence gap</span>

    <p>To understand the problem, we categorized SMBs into four decision-maturity profiles. The distribution reveals that the vast majority operate in what we call <span class="highlight">Dark Mode</span>: taking critical decisions without structured analytical support.</p>

<section class="post-section">
  <div class="post-container prose">
    <span class="section-number fi">04 · The structural solution</span>
    <h2 class="section-title fi">The structured decision framework for Brazilian companies</h2>
    <p class="section-lead fi">The consistent solution runs through three layers: People, Process and Technology.</p>

    <div class="framework-grid fi">
      <div class="framework-cell">
        <div class="framework-icon">01</div>
        <div class="framework-name">People</div>
        <div class="framework-desc">Whoever takes the decision needs an interpretation framework, not just access to data. Managers fail not from lack of available data, but from not having the architecture to turn it into a decision.</div>
        <span class="framework-tag">Training &middot; Access</span>
      </div>
      <div class="framework-cell">
        <div class="framework-icon">02</div>
        <div class="framework-name">Process</div>
        <div class="framework-desc">Every analysis needs a protocol: which sources, in what order, with what weight. Without process, the manager consults 3 isolated data points and calls it market research.</div>
        <span class="framework-tag">Methodology &middot; Structure</span>
      </div>
      <div class="framework-cell">
        <div class="framework-icon">03</div>
        <div class="framework-name">Technology</div>
        <div class="framework-desc">The data stack already exists: public reports, APIs, demographic bases, competitor data. The problem is the absence of integration and interpretation on top of scattered data.</div>
        <span class="framework-tag">Integration &middot; Interpretation</span>
      </div>
    </div>

    <p>D.Lab Experts applies this structure covering the full spectrum of questions a company needs to answer: <span class="highlight">who is the real competitor, who is the audience, how big is the market, where is the opportunity, and what's blocking performance</span>.</p>

    <span class="section-number fi">05 · The market</span>
    <h2 class="section-title fi">Why this market has not yet been solved in Brazil</h2>
    <p class="section-lead fi">The existing solutions were built for a client different from the SMB manager.</p>

    <div class="chart-card">
      <div class="chart-title">Landscape of business-intelligence solutions in Brazil</div>
      <div class="chart-desc">Positioning by target client size and estimated monthly cost</div>

    <p>The business-intelligence market in Brazil is dominated by enterprise players: corporate BI, strategy consultancies, data platforms. The smallest relevant client has a budget of <span class="highlight">R$ 5 to 15 thousand per month</span>, or pays R$ 50k+ for a project that delivers 80 slides with no actionable recommendation.</p>

    <div class="callout">
      <span class="callout-label">Market opportunity</span>
      <p>There are <span class="highlight">1.3 million new companies a year</span> in Brazil. And tens of millions of critical decisions taken without data. The data that answers those questions exists and is largely free. <strong>The product that turns it into decisions does not yet exist for those who need it most.</strong></p>

  <div class="post-container">
    <span class="section-number fi">06 · Methodology</span>
    <h2 class="section-title fi">Methodological notes</h2>

    <div class="methodology-box fi">
      <h3>About the data in this research</h3>
      <p>This research is secondary and exploratory in nature. The quantitative data was compiled from public sources (SEBRAE, IBGE, RAIS/MTE, FGV-EAESP) combined with D.Lab Experts primary analysis, including interviews with 80 managers and entrepreneurs in the São Paulo metropolitan area, conducted between 2024 and 2025.</p>
      <p>The maturity and cost estimates have a margin of +/-5 percentage points. They should not be interpreted as census or representative-sample research. The maturity-profile classification is a D.Lab Experts framework, not a taxonomy consolidated in the literature.</p>
      <p>The survival chart is an estimation model, not a controlled longitudinal study. A controlled longitudinal study is a D.Lab Experts objective for 2027.</p>
